### How a frame is drawn

- **Time.** `useCurrentFrame()` and `fps` give the source time. The pure evaluator then gives absolute poses, which
  are applied to the scene nodes (`Figure.W-C`, `W-RW`, `E-G`, `E-RD`, `E-LD`, `Puck`) in a layout effect before
  ThreeCanvas draws the frame.
- **No hidden state.** Nothing is integrated or carried between frames. The composition uses no `useFrame()` and
  no wall-clock time; the source test forbids them. R3F still creates its own internal `THREE.Clock`, which the
  shot never reads.
- **Untraced figures.** The seven figures not in the trace keep the static assembly pose (the trace's "others").
- **Angles.** Each pose matrix is built directly from the continuous heading. Nothing interpolates rotations
  between frames, so no turn can reverse through 0 deg.
  - In a 1 ms sweep, every figure's matrix heading equals its continuous heading.
  - No figure's heading jumps by 5 deg or more between samples.
  - W-RW's heading never decreases through the pass (counter-clockwise).
- **Version gate.** The shot does not render in any of these cases:
  - the trace's geometry version differs from `data/geometry.json` (0.6.0);
  - any asset hash in the trace differs from the manifest;
  - the trace is not `accepted`;
  - the GLB the browser actually loads hashes differently from the manifest (`cancelRender`).

  The current trace passes all of them. The tests show that a changed geometry version or a changed skater hash is
  rejected.
- **Read-back.** After applying the poses, the scene nodes are read back and compared with the pure state
  (`readback_max_diff`, 3e-14 mm/deg). A difference above 0.001 cancels the render.
- **Meshes.** The figure meshes in the scene GLB have the same bounding boxes as the meshes the iteration-22
  contact checks used (skater at k 1.0814, goalie at k 1.1422; checked once by reading the GLB).

## Limitations

- **Static figures.** The seven figures not in the trace stand in the static assembly pose. Their positions in
  the recording were not observed.
- **Renderer.** WebGL runs on the CPU (SwiftShader). There are no shadows, and the end screens are opaque. Seen
  from directly above, the goal cage roof hides most of the puck once it is in the net.
- **Inherited from the trace.** The far-corner shot direction is assumed. The puck brushes W-C's skate by 2.65 mm.
  The puck's resting place in the net is assumed. Contact dimensions are the preview values.
- **Proof clip resolution.** The clip is half resolution (`scale 0.5`) to save CPU time. The composition itself is
  1920 × 1080.
- **Frame rate.** 30 fps is a presentation choice; physical time comes from frame / fps at any rate.
